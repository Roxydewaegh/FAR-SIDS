FAR-SIDS labour scheme ledger

The schemes under which people from Jamaica, Tonga and Comoros go abroad to work on terms arranged before they leave. These belong to the remittance side of the study, not the aid side: a household member recruited under one of these schemes becomes a sender. It serves question A2c of the household survey, which asks how many family members abroad left on such a scheme, and it supplies the real names the question is asked with. A scheme makes the pick list only where the destination government, or an agreement between governments, sets the places or the terms, because that is what makes the flow of workers a cause rather than a consequence of what happens at home.

    Are the schemes aid? The live argument

    The destination governments place these schemes inside their own development policy. New Zealand's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade lists labour mobility under its aid and development programme, and Australia frames PALM within its Pacific development strategy.

    The scholarship has read them the same way for twenty years. Kapur (2004, page 10) wrote that immigrants, rather than governments, become the biggest provider of foreign aid; Curtain, Dornan, Doyle and Howes (2016, page xi) put the income gain from Pacific labour mobility at five to ten times the value of Australia and New Zealand's aid to the region; Huckstep and Beynon (2024) propose counting the remittances that new labour mobility places generate as mobilised climate finance; and Huckstep and Dempster (2025) record that remittances and aid often substitute for each other.

    Against that reading stands the accounting ruling. The OECD Development Assistance Committee has ruled, on PALM specifically, that migrants sending remittances home is not in itself sufficient for a programme to qualify as official development assistance, and the Addis Ababa Action Agenda (2015, paragraph 40) states that remittances cannot be equated to it. The study therefore records transfers arising from these schemes by the same four facts as every other transfer, assigns them to one flow by a written rule fixed before fieldwork, and computes every result a second time with the assignment flipped, so a conclusion that depends on the filing is reported as a finding rather than hidden inside one.

    The pick list names are confirmed with the key informants and the pilot before the A2c wording is frozen. Where a scheme touches the aid roster its record says so; everywhere else the two registers describe different machinery: aid programmes pay for things on the household payment map, and labour schemes recruit the people who become senders.
